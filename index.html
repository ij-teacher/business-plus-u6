01 / GRAMMAR FOCUS · P. 47

Comparisons for better choices

Comparing people, places, and things

Comparing two things

adjective + -er + than
more + adjective + than

Short adjectives often take -er. Longer adjectives often use more.

cheap → cheaper · big → bigger
comfortable → more comfortable

Comparing a group

the + adjective + -est
the most + adjective

Superlatives usually take the and describe the highest degree within a group.

good → better → best
bad → worse → worst

Equal and unequal comparisons

as + adjective + as
not as + adjective + as

Use the base adjective to say that things are equally or less so.

This room is as comfortable as that one.

Travel partners · Preferences, reasons, and advice

Work with a partner. Ask about their vacation preferences, explain your choices with because, then suggest a trip that suits them.

Ask your partnerAnswer patternIdeas and reasons
Where do you like to spend your vacation? Why?I like to spend my vacation at / in … because it is …at the beach · in the mountains · in a city · in the countryside
quiet · relaxing · exciting · interesting
What do you like doing on vacation? Why?I like / love / enjoy + -ing because I like to …backpacking · camping · skiing · going on coach trips · taking language courses
meet people · play sports · improve my English · have fun
How do you like to travel? Why?I like to travel by … / on foot because it is …by bus · by car · by coach · by train · by plane · by bicycle · on foot
fast · cheap · comfortable
Where do you like to stay? Why?I like to stay in / at / with … because it is …in a hotel · in a guest house · at a campsite · with friends
comfortable · relaxing · interesting
What would you suggest for your partner?You should / could + base verb.go to the beach · go backpacking · go camping · take the train · stay in a guest house

One conversation, three steps

  1. Ask and explain. Take turns asking the four preference questions. Give a complete answer and a reason each time.
  2. Listen and advise. Choose one suggestion that matches your partner’s interests. Use should for advice or could for an option.
  3. Report back. Tell the class one thing you learned about your partner and your suggestion.

Model conversation

A: Where do you like to spend your vacation?
B: I like to spend my vacation at the beach because it is quiet.
A: What do you like doing on vacation?
B: I love backpacking because I like to meet people.
A: You could go backpacking along the coast.
B: That sounds interesting!

Use these patterns to make your own answers. The model is a supplementary classroom example.
