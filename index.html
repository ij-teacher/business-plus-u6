01 / GRAMMAR FOCUS · P. 47

Comparisons for better choices

Comparing people, places, and things

Comparing two things

adjective + -er + than
more + adjective + than

Short adjectives often take -er. Longer adjectives often use more.

cheap → cheaper · big → bigger
comfortable → more comfortable

Comparing a group

the + adjective + -est
the most + adjective

Superlatives usually take the and describe the highest degree within a group.

good → better → best
bad → worse → worst

Equal and unequal comparisons

as + adjective + as
not as + adjective + as

Use the base adjective to say that things are equally or less so.

This room is as comfortable as that one.

One conversation, three steps

  1. Ask and explain. Take turns asking the four preference questions. Give a complete answer and a reason each time.
  2. Listen and advise. Choose one suggestion that matches your partner’s interests. Use should for advice or could for an option.
  3. Report back. Tell the class one thing you learned about your partner and your suggestion.

Model conversation

A: Where do you like to spend your vacation?
B: I like to spend my vacation at the beach because it is quiet.
A: What do you like doing on vacation?
B: I love backpacking because I like to meet people.
A: You could go backpacking along the coast.
B: That sounds interesting!
